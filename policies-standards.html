Consistent standards for safe and accountable care

Policies and clinical standards help guide professional practice, patient safety, responsible use of resources and consistent hospital operations.

Policies, procedures and standards

Hospital policies and standards establish expectations for clinical care, staff conduct, patient rights, infection prevention, information handling, risk management and administrative processes. They should be approved by the appropriate authority, communicated to relevant staff and reviewed at suitable intervals.

!

Important: This page is an overview of common policy areas, not a repository of approved ANGAU policies. No document should be treated as an official hospital policy unless its authenticity, approval, version and current status have been confirmed by hospital management or the responsible health authority. Do not rely on this page as a substitute for clinical guidance or emergency instructions.

Common policy and standards areas

The following categories may help patients, staff and partners identify the type of approved document they need. Their inclusion does not confirm the existence of a specific policy at ANGAU.

♡

Patient rights and responsibilities

Information about dignity, privacy, respectful treatment, informed participation in care, complaints and appropriate patient responsibilities.

✚

Clinical practice and care

Approved clinical protocols, referral processes, documentation requirements and professional responsibilities for relevant services.

＋

Infection prevention and control

Measures addressing hand hygiene, appropriate protective equipment, cleaning, safe waste handling and prevention of healthcare-associated infections.

⚕

Medicines and clinical supplies

Authorised procedures for prescribing, dispensing, storage, administration and safe management of medicines and clinical supplies.

▤

Records and information privacy

Appropriate access, accuracy, secure handling, retention and disclosure of patient and administrative information.

◎

Quality, safety and risk

Incident reporting, risk assessment, review of service quality, corrective actions and continuous improvement processes.

♙

Workforce and professional conduct

Staff responsibilities, respectful workplace expectations, supervision, training and relevant professional standards.

▦

Finance, procurement and assets

Controls for purchasing, financial approvals, inventory, maintenance and accountable management of public resources.

↗

Emergency and business continuity

Approved arrangements for emergency response, escalation, service disruption and continuity of essential operations.

What a controlled policy document should show

To help prevent outdated or unauthorised documents from being used, a published policy should normally identify its owner and approval status.

●
Official title and identifierA clear document name and, where used, a controlled policy or procedure number.
●
Approval and effective dateThe authorised approving role or body, approval date and effective date.
●
Version and review dateA version number, review schedule and a method to identify superseded copies.
●
Scope and responsibilitiesWho the document applies to, what it covers and who is responsible for implementation.

Requesting an approved policy

Patients should ask the relevant hospital office for public-facing information. Staff should use the hospital’s authorised internal document-control process for clinical or operational procedures. If the status of a document is unclear, confirm it before relying on it.

Restricted clinical procedures, security arrangements and internal staff documents should not be uploaded to a public website without formal permission and a review for confidential information.
